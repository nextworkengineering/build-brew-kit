# Authentic copy (make it sound like a person)

This kit exists so a vibe-coded post doesn't look vibe-coded. The design half is handled by the templates and the grade. This file is the other half: the words. Text generated by an AI has a handful of tells, and once you can see them you can't unsee them. Strip them and the post reads like the Build Master actually wrote it, because they did.

Adapted from the open-source `humanizer` skill by blader (github.com/blader/humanizer, MIT), narrowed to the patterns that show up in short social copy.

## The rule above all others: only say what's true
The worst AI tell isn't a phrasing, it's a lie. If the copy states a fact the Build Master never gave you, it's wrong even if it sounds great.
- Use their exact word for what they built. If they say "a carousel generator," the headline says carousel generator. Never round it up to "an app."
- Never invent a cadence. "We run these every month" is a fabrication unless they told you they run these every month.
- Never inflate a number or add a "first ever" they didn't claim.
- If you're not sure it's true, ask, or leave it out. A plain true line beats a punchy invented one every time.

## The tells, and the fix
Real examples from the first test carousel (left), rewritten (right).

| Tell | Looks like | Write instead |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| **Fabrication** | "Nine builders. One app." (they built a carousel generator) | "Nine builders. One carousel generator, shipped." |
| **Invented recurrence** | "We run these every month. Come shape the next one." | "Want to run one in your city? I'll help you start." |
| **Negative parallelism** (not X, but Y) | "Not a demo night, a feedback night." | "A feedback night. We went through the product page by page and wrote down what to fix." |
| **Antithesis, dressed up** | "Most people just use the product. We sat in it and said what to fix." | "We sat with the product for a night and wrote down what to fix." |
| **Rule of three** (staccato triad) | "Nine builders. One app. A very long list of notes." | "Nine of us spent the night writing down everything that got in the product's way." |
| **Aphorism formula** | "A year ago I couldn't build any of this. NextWork got me here, and now I get to host it." | "A year ago I couldn't build. NextWork changed that." |

Also cut, when they slip in:
- **Filler.** "in order to" becomes "to". "the fact that" just goes.
- **Rhetorical openers.** "Honestly?", "Let's be real", "Here's the thing".
- **Model vocabulary.** "testament", "landscape", "delve", "tapestry", "actually".
- **Em and en dashes.** "—" and "–" are banned. Use a period, a comma, or "and". A stray "—" is the single loudest tell.

## Two passes, always
1. Draft the lines from what they told you.
2. Before Gate 1, read every board line and the caption back and ask: would this obviously read as AI? Rewrite any line that hits a tell above, in the way the Build Master actually talks.
3. Before export, do it once more. Tells sneak back in on edits.

## The quick test
Read the line out loud. If it sounds like a person telling a friend what happened, keep it. If it sounds like a caption template or a LinkedIn post trying to be clever, it's a tell. When in doubt, say the plain true thing.
